So the prime clause sums of the solved pages (row lp1-prime-sums, z 7.9) are an authored property, and the transliteration convention for sums is confirmed. Consequence: the prime-sum check (work/prime_check.py) is a verifier Cicada itself used; any LP2 candidate's short clauses should be prime about 60% of the time against about 15% by chance.

**New tool from lp_verify.py: the A/I oracle.** LP2 has 100 one-rune words; in the solved pages one-rune words decrypt to A or I in 26 of 28 cases (92.9%; the author's figure, to be reproduced with work/lp.py), against 2/29 = 6.9% by chance. A true key therefore maps about 90% of LP2's one-rune words to A or I. Uses: (1) a verifier for any candidate (with the prime-sum check), (2) a near-free screening statistic for whole key families: at the 100 one-rune positions a random key passes about 7, a true key about 90, so it separates far beyond any IoC screen and needs no language model. Caveat: needs the key position at those runes, i.e. the stream and offset (and the skip drift), so it suits stream screens at known offsets, and it assumes LP2's plaintext keeps English's one-letter words.
