### 1.3 Findings (all new to the repository unless stated)

- **I1. The black glyph on image 39 is the Russian cross of images 17-19, rotated 90 degrees** (same three bars, same small square mark at each bar centre, arrow-like right end; `work/phase60/cross_vs_glyph.jpg`). Phase 9 listed it only as "a black glyph"; the wiki (Symbols page) lists the cross at its pages 1-3 and 23 (its numbering is one off from ours), so the return of the cross is known there. So the cross opens A1 and closes A4 (with a body strip on the same page), and the five-dot figure opens A5 right after (40). Known and re-measured: the five-dot figure is one rigid shape at 24, 40 (flipped) and 73 (rotated) (phase 9).
- **I2. The body strip occurs at 14, 15, 16 (LP1's last pages) and at 39.** In LP1 it closes the solved block; at 39 it closes A4. Reading: "end of a block" marker, not a key.
- **I3. Art is page-invariant inside a part** (phase 9: mirror residual exactly zero on 20-24, 32-38, 45-48, 51-56; the vines of 57-72 are identical). So art cannot be a per-page key stream; it can only select a method or a key per part.
- **I4. LP1's rule (b) fails in LP2.** A7 carries numerals (wedges, red 1-5) and A8 base-60 grids, A4 a grid, but all their ciphertext is flat (`obs-flat`). Numerals in a part therefore do not mark plain text, and no LP2 part looks plain.
- **I5. Layout is uniform.** Full LP2 pages are 12 lines of 21-25 runes (max 277 per page); partial pages are exactly those that end a segment or hold art/numerals. No indentation, line-count or glyph anomaly beyond the marks above. The rune lines wrap inside words, as `lp.word_spans` already assumes.
- **I6. Atbash/spiral parallel**: LP1's two Atbash sections both carry spirals; LP2's spiral parts are A2 and A8. Any Atbash-family key is a sign flip, which the 28-multiplier screens cover (`multipliers`, `chanscreen-step3`, both signs); a page-wise rule that applies pure Atbash only to some pages is excluded by the flat unigrams of every section.

Art -> LP1 method matches proposed and what covers them: spirals -> Atbash (above); leaf and tree art -> Vigenere with a solved-text keyword (`solved-phrase-keys`, `keywords-r4`: negative); cicadas on A8 and the hash page -> phi(prime) as An End (`keywords-r4` row: 4 channels, 48 windows, max -62.5); body/cross/dots -> block markers (restarts tested: `restart-units`, `restart-library`, `typo-restarts`; reuse between blocks is `key-reuse`, untestable).

## 5. Item 4: is the extra rune a simple function of the others? (`work/clue_extra_rune.py`, `results/analysis/phase60_extra_rune.txt`)

Test: in each of LP2's 2,896 words (2,365 of at least 3 runes), does some rune j equal (target + b) mod 29, for 15 targets x b in {-1, 0, +1} = 45 tests? Targets (rune index and Gematria prime-value domains): sum of the other runes, minus that sum, the same with prime values, the previous word's index sum / prime sum / length / last rune / first rune, the word's index in LP2 and in its section, its length, the position j, length - j, previous word's sum + this word's length. Statistic: number of hit words; null: 2,000 shuffles of all runes over the positions (word boundaries, lengths and rune frequencies kept), also used for the P of the maximum over the 45 tests.

**Result: negative.** Max z +2.08 ("position in word, b = +1"), P(max over 45 tests) = 0.58; the next four are z +1.77, +1.75, +1.63, +1.47. A deficit at one test is the known doublet suppression: "equals the previous word's last rune" 316 hits against 380 expected (z -3.5; deficit 64 words = the gap-doublet deficit of `obs-doublets`, not new).

## 8. Second follow-up, 2026-10-22: XOR-domain keys and the megabyte Cicada OS files under R4

### 8.1 XOR-domain keys (T7): defined exactly, then screened; only R4-free XOR is excluded

**Can the existing engines express it?** No: kscreen, chanscreen and the book decoder are additive mod 29 (a key value shifts the rune index), and 29 symbols are not closed under XOR of 5-bit numbers, so a XOR cipher needs a rule for results >= 29. Three exact conventions, all invertible given the plaintext (scripts `work/phase60_t7_xor.py` and `work/phase60_t7b_xor_classes.py`, a small numpy screen):
- **X1** index XOR, wrapped: c = (p XOR k) mod 29, p = rune index 0..28, k = key value 0..31.
- **X2** prime-value XOR, wrapped: c = (P[p] XOR k) mod 29, P[p] the rune's Gematria prime (2..109), k = key value 0..127.
- **X3** index XOR, pass-through: c = p XOR k when that is <= 28, otherwise c = p (the decoder sees c XOR k >= 29 exactly in the pass-through case, so it is invertible without ambiguity).
- **X4** XOR with rejection (the encryptor skips a key value whenever p XOR k >= 29) is NOT screened: which keys were skipped depends on the plaintext, so the key pointer cannot be recovered without a language model, and an LM-free statistic does not exist (a decoder that may skip at every step is always consistent). It is the same blind spot as the additive skip rule's pointer drift, with a 9% skip rate.

**First attempt (T7a), kept as a control result only.** A likelihood screen with an English unigram prior and a lag DP for R4's unkeyed extra runes (per rune LLR = log 29 P(c|k), at most 8 unkeyed runes in the first 120). Plants recovered at the true offset: X1 1 of 10, X2 3 of 10, X3 3 of 10 (`results/analysis/phase60_t7_xor_dp_control.txt`): wrapped XOR leaves almost every (c, k) pair explainable (density of non-empty preimages 0.92 for X1, 0.75 for X2), and a unigram prior gives about 0.3 nat per rune, which the insertions eat. Not run on LP2.

**T7b, the screen that was run: key-class coincidences.** Convention-free for every cipher in which one key value selects one substitution of the alphabet (all of X1-X3): at key offset o, group the 300 window runes by the key value they would be keyed with; two runes with the same key value share the substitution, so c_i = c_j occurs at the English coincidence rate (about 6.4%) instead of 3.4%. z = (pairs with equal key and equal ciphertext - n_c f) / sqrt(n_c f (1 - f)), n_c the ciphertext-equal pairs of the window, f the exact share of equal-key pairs at that offset. Strict alignment (no unkeyed rune).
- **Controls** (English plants through each convention, library streams as keys, random offset): without insertions 6 of 8 (X1), 8 of 8 (X2), 5 of 8 (X3) detected with z at the true offset >= 6.5 (median 11.6 / 9.4 / 9.8; random-stream maxima 5.9 / 5.4 / 4.7); **with R4's extra rune in 45% of the words 0 of 8 for all three (z about 0)**. So this is an R4-free test: it excludes XOR keys only where the key pointer is not disturbed (`results/analysis/phase60_t7b_control.txt`).
- **Library** (1,439 streams, every offset, the first window of each of the 8 section starts, 103.2 M trials; the prime-value relabelling of a stream has the same partition, so it adds no cells): best z 6.82 (`sha256-chain mod :an end`, 0.9, offset 16646); histogram z 3-4: 237,847; 4-5: 14,845; 5-6: 661; 6-7: 28; > 7: none. The Poisson tail of the pair count (about 53 expected pairs per cell) puts 0.1 values >= 6.8 among 10^8 trials, and a random-stream null of 6.4 M trials reaches 5.9. Decoding that window at the offset gives IoC 1.15 (X1) and 1.01 (X3), no language.
- **Cicada's number strings and onion strings** (30 keys: the whitespace sequences, digit strings, cookie values, the seven 256-byte strings and four XOR combinations; five key partitions each: value, mod 29, & 31, & 127, high five bits; periodic streams): best z 4.95 against a random-stream null of 6.76 (`phase60_t7b_strings.txt`).
- **The megabyte files** (560.17, _560.00, forwards and backwards, key = byte & 31 for X1 / X3 and byte & 127 for X2, every offset, 8 windows, about 83 M trials per partition): best z 6.21-6.93 over the eight cells; decoding the eight best candidates over their whole windows gives IoC 0.97-1.18 (`phase60_t7b_verify.txt`, X2 decoded as X1 / X3 only).
