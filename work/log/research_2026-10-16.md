## 2. Findings from today that are new relative to those sources

1. **The shared transcription has a rune error.** Page 8 line 5 is ᛒᛖᛚᛁᛖᚢᛖ (BELIEVE) in the image, but ᛒᛖᛚᛖᛁᚢᛖ in the
   rtkd/iddqd master. All community lineages share that root (Dukotah: 13,136/13,136 identical), and the community
   translation prints "BELEIVE". An OCR model trained on every other page (never on page 8) independently reads
   BELIEUE. The page is a solved plaintext page, so no key changes.
2. **The 256-byte base-60 payload (pp. 64–66) has five byte errors in the rtkd master, three of them also in
   Dukotah's `canon_256.bin`.** I checked them by glyph metrics (Futura: lowercase l rises above capital height,
   capital I does not; L has a foot; O is wider than 0) and confirmed every one by eye:
   - byte 45: `1L` (81) → **`1l` (107)**. Also wrong in Dukotah's canon.
   - byte 50: `0L` (21) → **`0l` (47)**. Also wrong in Dukotah's canon.
   - byte 246: `3i` (224) → **`3I` (198)**. Also wrong in Dukotah's canon (no dot, capital height).
   - byte 165: `0l` (47) → `0I` (18). Already right in Dukotah's canon.
   - byte 172: `2s` (174) → `2S` (148). Already right in Dukotah's canon.
   - Their **six "contested" bytes (25, 175, 182, 199, 215, 237) all confirm the existing reading**, which agrees
     with their own later resolution.

   **Why it matters:** Dukotah's lane B-05 uses the payload as a pseudo-random-function seed. Their own control
   shows a single wrong byte drops a correct key from −4.170 to −7.38, i.e. to noise. So every
   payload-as-seed negative computed on the old canon (Campaigns VII, IX, XX, B-05) is invalid until rerun on the
   corrected bytes. The corrections are in `ref/lp_verified.txt` and `ref/errata.tsv`.
3. **Dukotah's P-8 (haplography audit) is answered: no merged doublets.** On the unsolved pages, 74 of 82
   within-line doublets sit on lines whose image glyph count equals the transcription's. The 10 lines where the
   image has one more glyph are all explained: 5 are the list numerals 1–5, 1 is the "7" glyph, and 4 are vine
   twigs beside line 4 on pages 23/25/27/29, confirmed by eye. That is 0 merges against their pre-registered bar of
   ≥ 20 to falsify and < 5 to confirm, so **the anti-repeat filter is real**. (A label-free bitmap-similarity
   variant was inconclusive, because some different runes are near-identical in pixels.)
4. **An independent image-level re-read of LP2** (Phase 2 OCR, cross-validated) confirmed 12,955 of 12,956
   unsolved runes, with the last one (a drop cap) checked by eye. Dukotah's 2026-10-07 template lane reached
   the same conclusion independently (0.9973 per-slot agreement), so the two re-reads corroborate each other.
5. **Our skip-plus-missed-check screens** (numbers, keywords, hash/PRNG seeds, reverse direction) cover a
   decoding model that Wulfic's C14 does not, and that Dukotah handles with its own beam decoder.

## 3. External claims worth verifying here
- **aldegonde, within-word d = 5 coincidence excess: independently reproduced.** With line-wrapped words
  rejoined (our segmentation had split them; fixed in `lp.word_spans`), our verified data gives exactly
  **102/2,073 = 4.92%**. The boundary-permutation null is 76.6 ± 8.2, with 0 of 1,000 permutations ≥ 102
  (p < 0.001). The d = 6 dip is also reproduced (31 vs 44.4), and d = 4 leans high (131 vs 113.8, p = 0.03).
  **Implication:** a keystream generated independently of the plaintext (a true pad, or any PRNG including
  PHP/glibc/.NET) cannot make coincidences align with plaintext word boundaries. Together with the DJU-BEI
  repeat, this is moderate evidence (about 3.5σ) against the whole pad family, and for a cipher whose state
  follows word structure (aldegonde's "length-clocked walk").
- **aldegonde, repeated phrase ᛞᛄᚢ-ᛒᛖᛁ: reproduced.** It occurs at offsets 6555 and 12950 of our unsolved stream,
  and it is the only repeated 6-gram. About 1% under their null. If real, it suggests a key state that can recur, which argues against a position-unique pad.
- These are the only claimed non-random structures in LP2 besides the doublet deficit. Dukotah's negative-control
  battery has the d5 echo surviving at p = 0.020.

## 4. Open leads, ranked by what we can actually do
1. **PHP `mt_rand` sweep (Dukotah P-4, "highest-prior open generator").** It was blocked on having no PHP binary.
   This machine has PHP 8.2.12, and `MT_RAND_PHP` reproduces the pre-7.1 buggy generator, so a Rust port can be
   validated against real output and then swept over all 2³² seeds with a skip-aware decoder. Also uncovered:
   .NET `System.Random` (Knuth subtractive), which a C# re-implementation could cover.
2. **Rerun payload-as-seed tests on the corrected 256 bytes** (hash, KDF, PRF seeds). Our quick hash and AES
   checks on the corrected bytes are negative so far.
3. **Test aldegonde's word-scoped models** (length-clocked walk) once the d5 echo is reproduced.
4. **Dukotah P-9:** 47 unread ornament bands (prior low).
5. **Marsaglia Random Number CD-ROM (1995)** as a public pad: named, found and unswept.
