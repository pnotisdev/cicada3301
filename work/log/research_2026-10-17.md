# Research round 2: new public information, hints and candidate generators (2026-10-17)

Scope: LP2 (segments 0.7-0.15, 12,956 runes). Nothing in LP2 is solved. This note builds on
`work/log/research_2026-10-16.md` and does not repeat it. Every web page was read as data. No third-party code was
run, downloaded or installed. Quotes are short phrases only. Anything labelled MINE is a check I ran on our own files
with scratch scripts that are not in the repository; the method is described so it can be redone.

Numbering used below: **image number** = `work/orig/NN.jpg`. Community "LP2 page n" = image n + 17
(e.g. LP2 page 4 = `21.jpg`; the unsolved text starts on `17.jpg`; An End = `73.jpg`). The fan wiki's numbers are one
higher than that from about LP2 page 23 on (see section 7), so page numbers from the web must be checked against
the images.

## 0. Summary

1. **Almost nothing in the web material is independent of the same transcription lineage.** The 86 doublets, 0.66%,
   IoC 1.000 appear in every repository. They agree with our own OCR re-read, so they are real, but they are one
   measurement repeated. Most 2026 repositories are self-published, several are openly AI-assisted, and none has a
   verified solve.
2. **New and useful (MINE, verified):** the page images carry **tick marks the transcription lacks**. Four lone ticks
   (images 21, 38, 52, 58) look like apostrophes and 14 adjacent tick pairs look like quotation marks. The solved
   koans carry the same marks (ground truth). This confirms an aldegonde claim and gives usable cribs (section 2.6).
3. **New framing worth a cheap test:** a *relative-rank (chain) cipher*, c_i = c_{i-1} + 1 + (plaintext + key mod 28).
   It makes the doublet deficit structural instead of a bolted-on filter. Our c-domain keystream screens are
   **blind to this whole family** (section 4, candidate C1).
4. **Page art** (coordinator's extra item): the six cuneiform-style signs are Babylonian sexagesimal numerals and read
   (by my eye, and by an independent fan-wiki claim) as **17,13 / 55,1 = 1033 and 3301 in base 60**. They are a
   pixel-identical stamp, so they carry no per-page information (section 7).
5. No Cicada message after April 2017 was found. A "January 2017" message could not be found at all; the 2016 hint
   is the January 2016 post (section 3).

### 2.1 The anti-repeat (doublet) deficit: refinements

- [Dukotah FINAL-SYNTHESIS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/master/liber-primus/FINAL-SYNTHESIS.md)
  (status 2026-07-29): a "soft anti-repeat filter, p_keep about 0.18". Lag-1 suppression 80.75%. Lag 2-8 shows no
  bleed (any suppression above 1.70% excluded at 95%). Cross-line doublets 4/86 = 4.65% against a 4.58% base, so there is
  **no per-line rule**. Their human-calligrapher attribution was retracted.
- [aldegonde stream-cipher-no-repeat](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/stream-cipher-no-repeat.md):
  additive C = P + K with a re-key accepted with probability 1/5 gives 1/145 = 0.69% against 0.664% observed (86 vs 89.3).
  The authors say the 1/5 was calibrated to the data, not predicted. Doublet spacing is Poisson, there are zero triplets.
- [aldegonde five-block-boundary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/five-block-boundary.md):
  a no-repeat rule applied only inside 5-rune units leaves 1 in 5 adjacent pairs unconstrained, which gives the same
  0.69% with no free parameter. It fails two tests (doublet positions are flat mod 5; doublets and lag-5 events do not
  share a grid, p about 0.006). Status "unresolved".
- [Zhang FINDINGS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Leo-Y-Zhang/LiberPrimusAnalysis/main/docs/FINDINGS.md): the step
  c_i - c_{i-1} is uniform on 1..28 and independent of c_{i-1} (chi-square 801 on 784 df); the IoC of the step stream is
  1.024, which is just the missing zero. Zero rate 0.66%.
- [aldegonde autokey-plus-substitution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/autokey-plus-substitution.md):
  on J = (c_i - c_{i-1} - 1) mod 28 the 28-symbol IoC is 0.03576 against 1/28 = 0.03571. The J stream is flat in kappa at
  every shift (max |z| about 3.8 over 6,475 shifts), in Friedman IoC for periods 2-120, in DFT, in CRT projections and in
  linear functionals up to d = 20. They also ran a GF(29)* multiplicative battery (Legendre, monomials, multiplicative
  lagged-Fibonacci taps), all flat. Bounded-context inner tables up to about 5 runes are excluded by simulation.
  **Status "unresolved"; two surviving models:** a uniform keystream plus a rare-rune "ditto", or a designed inner table
  with 6+ runes of context.

### 2.2 Word-length anomaly (independent confirmation)

[aldegonde word-length-keystream-and-boundaries](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/word-length-keystream-and-boundaries.md):
2-rune words are 15.9% of LP2 words against 24.2% in the solved register (z about +4.9), 5-rune words are +3.3 points
and 8-rune words +2.1 points; "the whole gap sits at length 2". Mean 4.42 (merge-lines convention), which is exactly our
corrected figure. The deficit persists under every line-break convention. Their model 5 found 19 of 21 clean
image-versus-transcription disagreements are **separators present in the image and missing from the transcription**
(see 2.6). Dukotah says an earlier word-length "language" signal was a line-wrap artefact (same correction as ours).

**MINE: can lost separators alone explain it?** Using `work/wordlen.py` and Emerson word lengths in GP runes, I
merged a 2-rune word with the following word with probability m (fitted). Best m = 0.40: G = 38.5 on 10 df
(p = 3e-5), against G = 227 for plain English. The existing "+1 rune in 40% of multi-rune words" model gives G = 19.9
(p = 0.03), and adding merges to it does not improve it (best m = 0). So **separator loss alone is not the best
fit**; the audit in 2.6 is still the way to settle it. No null control beyond the model comparison.

### 2.3 Distance-5 echo, d1/d4 pairs, DJU-BEI

- [aldegonde lag5-digraph-structure](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/lag5-digraph-structure.md):
  paired matches at separations 1 and 4 within 5-rune frames. T(5) = 57 against 30.7 +/- 6.5 (z = +4.1), but the family
  was chosen after looking: a family-blind scan gives p about 0.033. The d1 face is explained by English affixes passing
  through a period-5 same-alphabet echo; the **d4 face is unexplained** and may be scan noise.
- [rune-s-lag5-echo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/rune-s-lag5-echo.md):
  within words the d5 excess is carried partly by the rune S (11 observed against 1.75). Under base-scrambling models
  P(max >= 11) is 0.08-0.15, so it is unremarkable there. It does refute identity-preserving mechanisms.
- [d5-partial-alphabet-leak](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/d5-partial-alphabet-leak.md):
  leak fraction q about 0.72, bootstrap CI for the d5 IoC [1.15, 1.72], so a full leak is not excluded. "Likely
  undecidable" with this corpus (needs about 4x more pairs).
- **MINE, DJU-BEI position.** The two occurrences of the 6-gram (offsets 6555 and 12950) are both structurally special:
  the first begins **28 runes after the start of segment 0.13**, the second **is the last six runes of LP2**. A keyless
  observation only; the repeat is still a post-hoc 1% event.
- **MINE, key reuse at the DJU-BEI distance.** The distance is 6395 = 5 x 1279, outside the lags our earlier scans
  covered (6000). Coincidence count at lag 6395 is 199 against 226.2 expected (z = -1.84, and 6 of the 199 are the
  repeat itself). A window of +/-40 lags around 6395 (to allow skip drift) gives z = +0.95, and the same windows
  around 1279, 2558, 3837 and 5116 give z between -0.74 and +0.01. Over lags 6200-6700 the maximum |z| is 3.35, which is
  what 500 lags give by chance. **Negative: no key reuse at 6395 or multiples of 1279.** Control: the window statistic
  is the one used elsewhere; no planted-key control was run for this single-lag test, so it only excludes strong reuse.

### 2.6 Marks the transcription does not contain (image level)

This is the most useful new item. [aldegonde contraction-cribs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/contraction-cribs.md)
claims the images carry a raised tick glyph: four lone ticks that act as apostrophes (tails 'S, 'D or 'T) and fourteen
ticks in adjacent pairs acting as quotation marks, forming seven spans. [aldegonde mark-glyph-inventory](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/mark-glyph-inventory.md)
adds that line-initial marks are dropped from the transcription and that 13% of recorded '.' are not the 4-dot glyph.
Their own reader aligns only 89.8% of lines and they hand-checked 2 of 21 disagreements, so I verified it myself.

**MINE, method.** Binary threshold (grey < 110) of each image in `work/orig`, 8-connected components, keep
components 10-12 px wide, 38-42 px tall, area 340-385 px (the tick is a tapered stroke, area about 362). The tick is a
pixel-identical stamp, so the signature is sharp.

**Consequences.**
1. The plaintext of LP2 contains **dialogue and contractions in the same koan style as the solved pages**. That is
   weak but real evidence that the unsolved text is the same genre.
2. **Four cribs:** the rune after each lone tick is S, D or T; at images 21 and 58 (3-rune stems) it is probably n't,
   so plaintext N and T at two known offsets. These do not find a key (the skip rule shifts alignment), but they
   are a strong post-hoc filter: 3/29 per apostrophe site, 1/841 for an n't site.
3. **Seven quoted spans** (lengths 2-34 words) with known positions. In the solved koans a closing quote is followed
   by a dialogue tag (ASKED/REPLIED THE MASTER, HE SAID), which has a recognisable word-length pattern; with 7 spans
   the power is low.
4. The transcription's **separator layer is unverified** (our Phase 2 verified runes only). Any word-scoped statistic
   (d5 echo, DJU-BEI, `walk`, `dsearch`, word-length) rests on it.

The ruled-out list in `PLAN.md` is large. After the web read, the **only family I can find that is not covered and
has a concrete reason to fit the fingerprint** is the chain / relative-rank family (C1). C2 is its large-compute

### C1. Relative-rank (chain) cipher over the 28 non-previous runes, keyed in the *step* domain

Source of the idea: [Zhang REPORT](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Leo-Y-Zhang/LiberPrimusAnalysis/main/docs/REPORT.md)
("chain cipher c_i = c_{i-1} + s_i, s_i in 1..28") and [aldegonde first-difference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cicada-solvers/aldegonde/main/hypotheses/first-difference.md),
which states the *keyed* chain model is left untested. Zhang's one-line model does not say where the plaintext enters;
the version below is mine.

**Algorithm.** Index the 29 GP runes 0..28. Let pi map 28 plaintext classes to Z28 (unknown bijection, any plaintext
language) and K_i a keystream value in Z28. Encrypt:

    s_i = (pi(p_i) + K_i) mod 28          # variant A: additive on the step index
    c_i = (c_{i-1} + 1 + s_i) mod 29      # never equals c_{i-1}

Variant B (multiplicative, the GF(29)* reading): s_i = pi(p_i) * K_i in GF(29)*, c_i = c_{i-1} + s_i. This is variant A
applied to discrete logarithms of s_i and K_i, so one implementation covers both with a relabelling of both streams
(key values that are 0 mod 29 have no logarithm and are dropped).
The 29th plaintext symbol (or a rare event) must be the "ditto" with c_i = c_{i-1}; that supplies the 86 surviving
doublets and is the weak point (see below).

**Why it fits the fingerprint.** Flat unigrams (the step is key-flattened); doublets absent by construction, with no
ad hoc rule and no skip desynchronisation; effect only at lag 1; independent of word boundaries; J flat and memoryless
(chi-square 801/784). The 5x suppression is then "a 1/29 chance minus the ditto rate" without a calibrated 1/5.

**Why every earlier screen is blind to it.** The ciphertext is c_i = c_0 + sum_{j<=i}(1 + pi(p_j) + K_j): a cumulative
sum of plaintext plus key, i.e. a random walk. A c-domain screen subtracts a stationary keystream from c, so even the
true key's own prefix sums leave the plaintext's random walk behind. Zhang ran only 22 sequences x 300 offsets in this
form, jens-wedin ran word and book keys in "difference space" (REPORT section 11), and aldegonde ran self-statistics
on J, not a keystream library. Our kscreen library (1,447 streams), all order <= 3
recurrences and the PRNG sweeps were never applied to J.

**Against it (be honest).**
(a) The ditto's positions are boundary-blind (0.63% inside words, 0.80% across gaps, [Zhang FINDINGS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Leo-Y-Zhang/LiberPrimusAnalysis/main/docs/FINDINGS.md)),
so the ditto cannot be a letter such as EA (word-start rate would differ); Zhang reports the EA reading fails.
(b) It says nothing about the d5 echo or DJU-BEI. (c) aldegonde excludes generic inner tables with up to about 5
runes of context; a keystream is not an inner table, so this does not touch C1.

**Cheap test.**
1. *Step 0, free, existing engine unchanged:* export windows of D_i = (c_{i+1} - c_i) mod 29 (zeros kept) to
   `work/kscreen/data` and run `kscreen-sections`. This covers the mod-29 chain c_i = c_{i-1} + p_i + k_i with the
   skip/coin models. About 19 minutes on the laptop. (Mod 29 does not explain the zero deficit by itself, so treat it
   as a coverage control.)
2. *Step 1:* add an alphabet-size parameter (N = 28) to the kscreen engine, window J_t = D_t - 1 for D_t != 0, and
   score the IoC of (J_t - K_{o+t}) mod 28 over 300-symbol windows at every offset, for every library stream reduced
   mod 28 (variant A) and the discrete-log relabelling (variant B). Run it two ways: zero steps dropped without
   consuming a key value, and zero steps consuming one. No beam is needed because the no-repeat rule is not a filter
   here. Then every order <= 3 recurrence over Z28 and Z29, and the PRNG streams.
3. *Positive controls:* Emerson text as GP runes, EA as the ditto, chain-encrypted with totient, pi base 28, SHA-256
   counter mod 28, primes mod 28, a polynomial key and a random key; require exact-offset recovery in at least 14 of
   15 plants at the section scale. **Blindness control:** run the existing c-domain kscreen on the same chain
   ciphertexts and show it recovers 0 of 15 (this is what justifies the new screen).
4. *Null controls:* 300 random keystreams per window, plus each library stream at 20 random offsets on the same
   window (the matched null already used for kscreen). IoC thresholds must be re-calibrated for 28-ary data and the
   depleted zero (baseline IoC about 1.024 on D, 1.000 on J).
5. *Decision:* the rules in `docs/CAMPAIGNS.md` (whole-window verification, matched control, second section).

Cost: about a day of engine work; compute of the same order as the existing `kscreen-sections` run.

### C2. PRNG sweeps in the J-domain and per section

The three engines in `work/phpmt` sweep 2^32 seeds on segment 0.7 in the c-domain. The J-domain variant
(K_t = rand() mod 28, step as above) is another set of variants per seed. This is the large-compute extension of C1.
It should wait for C1's library result and keep the gating rules of `docs/GPU_PORT.md`. A negative on structured
keystreams says nothing about a weak PRNG, which is why the sweep is separate. Unswept "compute-only tails" named by
[Dukotah PICKUP-HERE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/master/liber-primus/PICKUP-HERE.md): bash
`$RANDOM`, Perl 5.14 `rand` (drand48), TeX LCG, the Marsaglia CD-ROM.

(A literal-F pass-through, as in the solved sections, is not a candidate here: it would put ᚠ at about 5.8% of the
ciphertext in any domain, and LP2's ᚠ is 3.54%, `PLAN.md` Phase 3.)

## 5. Not recommended (and why)

- **Word-order transposition driven by "their numbers are the direction"**: doublet suppression is the same across
  word gaps in printed order (0.80%), so the printed order is the cipher order at least across gaps; Pitchfork's
  art-order transpositions are all NULL.
- **Digraph / Playfair**: bigram table uniform off the diagonal; disproved by aldegonde, Dukotah, Zhang.
- **Red incipits as formulaic cribs (MINE):** from our `work/tmp/phase4/red_runs.json` the word-length patterns of the
  red text at section starts are (8,5) in 0.7, (2,11,3) in 0.8, (2,6,3,5) in 0.12, (3,12,4) in 0.13 and (3,5) in
  0.15 (0.10's caption is (4,5)). The solved headings are short formulae (A KOAN = 1,4; AN INSTRUCTION = 2,10;
  SOME WISDOM = 4,6; KNOW THIS = 4,3), so LP2's red text is a different kind of title (2-4 words, 8-19 runes) and
  cannot be cribbed from the formula set. A 12-rune and an 11-rune word are unusual in English.
- **Periodic key at the DJU-BEI distance**: tested, negative (section 2.3).
- **The deep-web page as the pad:** the book names it, which is the most natural reading of "OTP-class", but it needs
  new data (the page itself); the previous Tor crawl corpora are version-2 onions only. Nothing to test here.

## 7. Page art, cuneiform signs and borders (coordinator's extra item)

Builds on `work/log/phase9_art.md`; only what is new is listed.

### 7.1 The six cuneiform-style signs (images 50 bottom, 51-56 top)

- **MINE, identity.** Over a 420 x 420 px region the cluster is **pixel-identical** on images 52-56 against 51
  (IoU 1.000), and found again at the bottom of image 50 (IoU 0.88 at the best alignment, a 2 px shift, so not an
  exact copy: perhaps redrawn or slightly scaled; I did not resolve which). I looked for it at the same top position
  on every page (IoU below 0.3 except 51-56) and with a sliding search on pages 49, 50 and 57 only. It therefore
  carries no per-page information.
- **MINE, reading.** The wedges are the two Babylonian numeral stems (corner wedge = 10, vertical wedge = 1).
  Top row: 10 + seven wedges, 10 + three wedges, i.e. **17, 13**. Bottom row: a lattice of five corner wedges (50) +
  five vertical wedges, then one vertical wedge, i.e. **55, 1**. In base 60 that is 17x60+13 = **1033** and
  55x60+1 = **3301**, Cicada's two numbers (1033 is the first magic square's sum on 0.2, 3301 is the page-14/16
  square constant and the group's number). The same reading is given independently by the fan wiki: it lists
  "34.jpg to 39.jpg" as cuneiform numbers and reads 17, 13, 55, 1 as 1033 and 3301
  ([Symbols/Drawing/Art of Liber Primus](https://uncovering-cicada.fandom.com/wiki/Symbols/Drawing/Art_of_Liber_Primus),
  seen only as a search snippet because the page itself returned HTTP 402). **UNVERIFIED by a cuneiform
  specialist**; the numerals are drawn as outlines with overlapping strokes, so the wedge counts are by eye.
- **Language?** No source reads them as text. Neither the wiki, [cicadasolvers.com's quickstart](https://www.cicadasolvers.com/quickstart/)
  ("cuneiform numerals", with Möbius strips, as page-specific original artwork) nor the Pitchfork notes suggests Old
  Persian, Akkadian, Sumerian or Elamite text. The Old Persian/Elamite hits in my searches were OCR papers, not
  commentary on this book. Old Persian signs would also look different (alphabetic wedge groups), not numerals.
- **Key or keyword?** The only link is symbolic. The numerals pair 1033/3301 with the *base-60* hint, and the 256-byte
  grid on images 66-68 uses two-digit base-60 tokens whose values reach 255 (our `work/grid60.py`), i.e. the same
  notation as "17,13". This supports reading the grid as base-60 numbers, which was already done; no source ties the
  cluster to a key, a keyword or the section's language. Pitchfork lists "a base-60 reading of the cuneiform section
  against the sexagesimal block" as untried; nothing is claimed.

### 7.2 Dots and other figures

- **Five-dot constellation.** Fan wiki: "5 dots (page 7, 24, 57)" and a guess that the dots resemble stars of Aquila,
  with the three dots as Orion's belt, explicitly unconfirmed. A [Steemit note](https://steemit.com/cicada3301/@j1337/cicada-3301-liber-primus-extra-notes-to-share)
  lists the pages as 7, 23, 56, which is our images 24, 40, 73 (so the wiki's later numbers are off by one).
  **MINE:** I located the five large dots (diameter 69-70 px) on exactly images 24, 40 and 73; the ten pairwise
  distances agree to 0.3 px between 24 and 40 (40 is 24 flipped vertically, dot y values sum to 3675) and to 2 px for
  73 (rotated or mirrored). One rigid stamp, as `phase9_art.md` found.
- **Three-dot rows.** Fan wiki: pages 34 and 40, our images 50 and 56. **MINE:** collinear dots at two scales; spacing
  417:478 px on image 50 and 196:224 px on image 56, ratio 2.13 on both gaps. I did not test star-chart matches; the
  belt of Orion is, from memory, almost evenly spaced (gap ratio near 1.04), which this row (1.146) does not match, and
  that figure should be checked in a catalogue before anyone relies on it. No source gives a cipher meaning for the dots.
- **Mirrored spiral/tree borders, insects.** The only public analyses are catalogues and guesses. Fan wiki: root and
  plant ornaments, a dead oak, a possible cicada wing, a mayfly on pages 15 and 25-27 and a cross on pages 1-3 and 23;
  its speculation is that the mayfly means an ephemeral key, spirals a route cipher, Fibonacci trees a Beaufort cipher,
  and that art divides the book into sections. [cicadasolvers.com's quickstart](https://www.cicadasolvers.com/quickstart/)
  says sections are "ostensibly indicated by original marginalia" and that the mayfly and oak tree are edited existing
  artwork. Attempts to *use* the art: Pitchfork-and-Torch tried spiral/ring order, leaf and branch order, rail-fence,
  direction reversal and base-60 transposition (all NULL_ATTACK, August-September 2026); Dukotah rounds 22-24 found the
  turtle-render, drop-cap, koan-acrostic and printed-line-geometry channels null, and rate the figural-motif channel
  (D2) as low-expectation and never run ([Dukotah PICKUP-HERE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/master/liber-primus/PICKUP-HERE.md));
  aldegonde's inventory treats 14- and 15-dot shapes on line 4 of six consecutive pages as ornaments (our "vine
  twigs", `research_2026-10-16.md`). Dukotah also pins the 2014 PDF to Ghostscript 9.01-9.15 and lists the art
  attribution as open. No source reports a key, keyword or plaintext from any of this.

### 7.3 MINE: the art changes where the transcription's segments change

The [Pitchfork inventory](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Pitchfork-and-Torch/liber-research/main/notes/uncracked-inventory.md)
labels the art by "onion" page ranges (onion n = our image n + 17): 0-2 Sign post and Cross, 3-7 Spirals, 8-14
Branches, 15-22 Möbius, 23-26 Mayfly, 27-32 Wing and Tree, 33-39 Cuneiform, 40-53 Spiral Branches, 54-55 Spiral and
Dead Tree. Mapped to our segments, **every label starts at a segment start**, except the 54-55 split that lies inside
0.15 (start images from `lp.load_segments`:
0.7 = 17, 0.8 = 20, 0.9 = 25, 0.10/0.11 = 32, 0.12 = 40, 0.13 = 44, 0.14 = 50, 0.15 = 57, An End = 73, Parable = 74).
I checked it on the pixels: margins of images 17-19 are identical, 20-24 are identical, 26/28/30 and 27/29/31
alternate, and 57-72 all share one margin dot pattern (IoU above 0.9 on a 4x-reduced margin image); image 14's border is
the mirror image of image 39's (score 1.00 with 2-px tolerance) and 10 mirrors 11 (0.84). The test has no power
for 0.10-0.14, whose pages are mostly empty margin, so those boundaries rest on the Pitchfork labels. This supports
the transcription's `$` segmentation as the author's own section structure and agrees with the earlier finding that
all nine sections show the same doublet deficit (0.52-1.08%): one output stage, not nine ciphers selected by art.
