## Top 3 untested leads, specified to run later

### T1. Do LP2's clause marks sit at plaintext clause ends? (from S1; reproduced here)
- Input: `work/ref/lp_verified.txt` through `work/lp.py load_segments()`; LP2 = segments 7-15, solved text = segments 0-6, 16, 17. Words: runes between ' ' tokens, joined across line and page breaks. Clause end: '.' or '&'.
- Statistics (all by script, fixed before running): (a) D = mean length of clause-final words minus mean of all other words; (b) the same for the first word and the penultimate word of each clause; (c) cv = sd / mean of words per clause; (d) D in clause-length bins (at most 13 words; 14-30; over 30) for LP2.
- Reference values already measured here: LP2 D -0.26 (z -1.4), cv 0.95; LP1 D +1.77 (z +7.3), cv 0.71. Discord quotes English +1.2.
- Positive control: English (`work/ref/emerson_essays1.txt`, `work/ref/english_20k.txt`) spelled in runes, cut at its own sentence marks, then passed through the R4 transform (repair at q 0.81, extra rune in 45% of words; engine from phase 51/57): D must stay at +1.2 to +1.8 and cv at 0.6-0.8. A second control: take LP1 and move each '.' to a random word gap keeping the clause count: D must fall to about 0 and cv to about 1 (this is the LP2 pattern).
- Null: 2,000 shuffles of the clause marks over the word gaps of LP2 (keep count 168); report D and cv percentiles. Also fit a Bernoulli placement (p per word gap) and compare the full clause-length histogram (chi-square).
- Decision rule: if D(LP2) is within the shuffle null and below the R4-on-English control by more than 4 sd, and cv matches the Bernoulli placement, record "LP2 clause marks are not at plaintext clause ends (or the plaintext is not clause-structured prose)". Consequences to write down: prime-clause verifier and the 45 clauses of 13-40 runes cannot be used as clause units on LP2; the A/I oracle (one-rune words) is unaffected.
- Follow-up if it holds: is the placement related to ciphertext events (last word before a '.' vs doublet sites, lag-6 sites, S,S sites)? Gap-doublet counts by gap type are above (1 of 153 at '.', 22 of 2,728 at ' ').

## Killed by known signal

- **S4, ozon's in-word-only filler insertion.** If equal runes were removed only inside words, the 2,895 word, clause and paragraph gaps would keep the random rate of 3.45%, about 100 gap doublets. LP2 has 23 (0.8%), against 63 inside words (0.63%). The post's rate argument (average word length 5-6) is beside the point; the gap count decides (`obs-doublets`, brief kill test: a per-word digraph cipher leaves gap doublets at 3.45%).
- **tz18's and Garrodactyl's ciphertext or cumulative autokey** (`c_k - c_{k-1} = p_k` after algebra: doublets exactly where the plaintext rune is 0). A mechanism that makes doublets on one plaintext rune cannot put 23 of 86 across word gaps (an earlier session recorded 2 of 2,977 word-initial NG in the solved pages; not rechecked here); it is also the step-domain cipher (`step28`) and `chain`. Mokzy reaches the same conclusion (needs a rare marker symbol).
- **S7, collision-triggered key reset to the key start** (interruptor idea). With a short key (period up to about 40) the stretches between resets (about 150 runes on average at 0.66% collisions) are ordinary periodic Vigenere; every lag up to 40 is flat (`obs-flat`) and periodic keys with skip drift were nulled (`periodic`). With a long key the reset has no visible effect and cannot be tested.
- **S10, AZdecrypt-style homophonic or monoalphabetic solving with nulls/skips.** A substitution layer keeps the plaintext letter frequencies in the unigrams; 29 symbols cannot flatten English (E alone is 12%), and LP2's unigram chi-square is 26.4 on 28 df (Mokzy's figure; ours in `obs-flat`). Nulls would have to be uniform on all 29 runes to keep it flat, which is the extra-rune model already in R4.
- **Digraph or Playfair cipher** (Scott, sltn, hyperstitionidae): bigrams are flat (chi-square 786 against 760 +- 45, `obs-flat`); a digraph cipher forbids only in-pair equals, so gap doublets would sit at 3.45%.
- **Mokzy's "no plaintext" generator (S5), partly.** A uniform generator with a soft anti-repeat rule and word lengths borrowed from LP1 would not give 2-rune words at 15.5% (LP1 24.1%), the lag-6 excess inside words (z +5.3), or a clause-length cv of 0.71 (LP2 0.95 is Bernoulli-like). It does fit S1, so it stays as a hypothesis, but it has nothing to decrypt.
- **heck's "I+M = 29" marker (S8), as a global mechanism.** LP2 as a whole has IM 18 and MI 20 against 15.4 each and sum-0 pairs 438 against 446.7; only the last 308 runes are enriched, and the pair was picked after looking.
